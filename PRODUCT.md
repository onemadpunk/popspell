# PopSpell: product purpose

This is the short product brief for planning changes to PopSpell. The [README](README.md) describes the current features and implementation; [teacher help](help.html) explains how to use them.

## Purpose

PopSpell helps pupils make progress in spelling, reading, writing and numeracy through clear teaching, practice and feedback. It gives teachers a practical way to find the right starting point, plan and assign work, see what a pupil can do, and decide what to teach next. Pupils can carry on with the same work at school and at home.

Sounds-first spelling is the foundation: pupils hear and segment a word, map sounds to letters, spell it, and receive feedback that explains the sound or pattern to revisit. Other literacy and maths work should follow the same principle of teaching a skill, giving meaningful practice and showing the teacher useful evidence.

## People and experiences

- **Pupils:** Children and young people using PopSpell on a shared school device or at home. The welcome screen works for either age group. Younger pupils have Pip and a playful look; older pupils have the 12–17 look. The work offered to a pupil can be above or below the level usually associated with their age.
- **Teachers:** Choose a class, assess starting points, select or make lessons and lists, assign work, print sheets, record paper results, and review progress. The teacher area is a planning and records workspace. A teacher normally sees their own classes; elevated roles may choose to view all classes.
- **Families:** Use a pupil's home link and the information a teacher chooses to share on a parent page. Families can support practice without seeing private teacher notes or other pupils' records.

## Product principles

1. **Match work to the pupil.** Year group is a starting guide. Starting checks, past work and teacher judgement determine the working level for each area. A pupil may be ahead in one subject and need earlier work in another. Filters should make suitable work easy to find without preventing a teacher from choosing a different level.
2. **Teach through feedback.** Show what the pupil did, explain the relevant sound, spelling pattern or mathematical idea, and offer a useful next step. Accuracy matters more than speed. Celebrate a pupil's own progress; keep games free of public rankings.
3. **Keep the teacher in control.** PopSpell can suggest work and summarise evidence. The teacher chooses assignments, confirms levels, marks paper work and makes educational judgements. Practice results alone are not a formal curriculum assessment.
4. **Make each age feel welcome.** Keep younger activities clear, spoken and playful. Use the older pupils' brand and suitable language for secondary work. Do not let a mascot or visual style make older pupils feel that the content is for much younger children.
5. **Support real classrooms.** Work on phones, tablets and computers; make touch and keyboard controls usable; retain the pupil app's offline practice; allow paper sheets and home practice; keep teacher tasks quick enough to use during a teaching day.
6. **Protect pupils' information.** Keep class and pupil data within the permissions of their teacher and authorised administrators. Show families only the information intended for them. Collect and expose only what a feature needs.
7. **Use curriculum labels carefully.** Scotland's Curriculum for Excellence and England's curriculum help teachers find and plan work. Labels and automated status summaries are guides to the content practised, with the teacher's wider evidence needed for any judgement.

## Scope for new work

A proposed feature should answer these questions before implementation:

1. Which pupil, teacher or family task does it improve?
2. What learning skill or teaching decision does it support?
3. How will it handle pupils working above or below their year group?
4. What will the pupil and teacher actually see, and what evidence will the teacher gain?
5. Does it fit both age presentations where relevant, accessibility, privacy and offline practice?

The current subject focus is spelling, reading, writing and numeracy, with Scotland and England curriculum support, including secondary lessons where the content exists. Additions outside that focus need an explicit product decision and an update to this brief. Feature counts and coverage should be checked against the current content and build checks before making claims about them.
