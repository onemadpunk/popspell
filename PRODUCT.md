# PopSpell: product purpose

This is the short product brief for planning changes to PopSpell. The [README](README.md) describes the current features and implementation; [teacher help](help.html) explains how to use them.

## Purpose

PopSpell helps pupils make progress in spelling, reading, writing and numeracy through clear teaching, practice and feedback. It gives teachers a practical way to find the right starting point, plan and assign work, see what a pupil can do, and decide what to teach next. Pupils can carry on with the same work at school and at home.

Sounds-first spelling is the foundation: pupils hear and segment a word, map sounds to letters, spell it, and receive feedback that explains the sound or pattern to revisit. Other literacy and maths work should follow the same principle of teaching a skill, giving meaningful practice and showing the teacher useful evidence.

## People and experiences

- **Pupils:** Children and young people using PopSpell on a shared school device or at home. The neutral name chooser works for either age group. Younger pupils have Pip and a playful look; older pupils have the 12–17 look. The work offered to a pupil can be above or below the level usually associated with their age. The usual way in is the class code plus the pupil's first name, or a tab the teacher opens for that pupil on their own computer (Steven's decision, November 2026). A class code and first name can fetch only that pupil's assigned work and send results; it cannot read back their records. A prepared tablet with name tiles and a four-digit PIN, or a personal home link with the same PIN, remains available where a teacher wants it.
- **Teacher flow:** The teacher signs in, picks the class and sees each pupil on one Class page: the work set for them, a button to open it for them in a new tab, and their recent results, each opening to show every word. Planning and record pages (Today, Next steps, Programme, Plans and sheets, Assign, Pupils, Diary) are under More tools.
- **Teachers:** Choose a class, assess starting points, select or make lessons and lists, assign work, print sheets, record paper results, and review progress. The teacher area is a planning and records workspace. A teacher normally sees their own classes; elevated roles may choose to view all classes.
- **Quick trials:** A teacher can let a child try one piece of work on the teacher's own device without a class, pupil login or PIN, for example at a first meeting. The teacher is signed out while the child works, so pupil records stay closed. The result is a dated trial record under the teacher's own account, kept apart from class records and progress, which the teacher reviews and deletes by hand. Automatic deletion after a retention period is planned for later.
- **Families:** Use a pupil's home link and the information a teacher chooses to share on a parent page. After a lesson the teacher can send the family that lesson's notes and practice, which appear on the parent page; plans and next lesson dates stay off it because the teacher may change them (Steven's decision, November 2026). Families can support practice without seeing private teacher notes or other pupils' records.

## Product principles

1. **Match work to the pupil.** Year group is a starting guide. Starting checks, past work and teacher judgement determine the working level for each area. A pupil may be ahead in one subject and need earlier work in another. Filters should make suitable work easy to find without preventing a teacher from choosing a different level.
2. **Teach through feedback.** Show what the pupil did, explain the relevant sound, spelling pattern or mathematical idea, and offer a useful next step. Accuracy matters more than speed. Celebrate a pupil's own progress; keep games free of public rankings.
3. **Keep the teacher in control.** PopSpell can suggest work and summarise evidence. The teacher chooses assignments, confirms levels, marks paper work and makes educational judgements. Practice results alone are not a formal curriculum assessment.
4. **Make each age feel welcome.** Keep younger activities clear, spoken and playful. Use the older pupils' brand and suitable language for secondary work. Do not let a mascot or visual style make older pupils feel that the content is for much younger children.
5. **Support real classrooms.** Work on phones, tablets and computers; make touch and keyboard controls usable; retain offline practice after sign-in while the page remains open; allow paper sheets and home practice; keep teacher tasks quick enough to use during a teaching day.
6. **Protect pupils' information.** Keep class and pupil data within the permissions of their teacher and authorised administrators. Show families only the information intended for them. Collect and expose only what a feature needs.
7. **Use curriculum labels carefully.** Scotland's Curriculum for Excellence and England's curriculum help teachers find and plan work. Scotland's refreshed Know, Do, Understand curriculum is planned for Early to Third Level from August 2028 and Fourth Level and the senior phase from August 2029; PopSpell should move to it once the frameworks are final (May 2027), and until then keeps the current codes. England follows the current national curriculum until the revised one is published. Labels and automated status summaries are guides to the content practised, with the teacher's wider evidence needed for any judgement.
8. **Follow the teacher's phonics programme.** The pupil journey follows the EA Literacy stages (Stages 1 to 3, then Stages 4 to 7, block by block) with the teacher's own word lists where she has them, so a stage or block number means the same on PopSpell as on the teacher's sheets. PopSpell's own practice is added at the end of the stage it fits, never as an extra stage.

## Scope for new work

A proposed feature should answer these questions before implementation:

1. Which pupil, teacher or family task does it improve?
2. What learning skill or teaching decision does it support?
3. How will it handle pupils working above or below their year group?
4. What will the pupil and teacher actually see, and what evidence will the teacher gain?
5. Does it fit both age presentations where relevant, accessibility, privacy and offline practice?

The current subject focus is spelling, reading, writing and numeracy, with Scotland and England curriculum support, including secondary lessons where the content exists. Additions outside that focus need an explicit product decision and an update to this brief.

**Quick drills** (Steven's request, November 2026): one-page drills modelled on the format of the tutoring sheets in Sara's folder (series strip, rule or word box, short numbered tasks, one skill per page), for the start or end of a tutoring session or as homework. They are paper sheets attached to ready lessons, so they sit in the Library and lesson packs rather than a new area, and each print draws a fresh mix so a drill can be repeated. Content is PopSpell's own; the third-party sheets are a guide to format and level only.

**Secondary maths for England** (Steven's request, November 2026): PopSpell was asked about maths for high school pupils in England, so Years 7 to 11 have online practice and quick drills as well as lessons and sheets. Years 10 and 11 practice follows the key stage 4 programme of study and the GCSE subject content (Foundation and Higher, with higher-tier questions marked); it is practice for the topics, not past or mock exam papers. Questions are PopSpell's own.

**GCSE maths exam climb** (Steven's request, November 2026): PopSpell was asked to prepare pupils for GCSE maths in England, for example a pupil at grade 1 who needs a grade 3 for college. It is built as a staged climb, working up the grades: stage 1 is Foundation, grade 1 to 3 (14 topics in "grade 3 first" order, a baseline, a tracker, a ready lesson, online practice and an exam-style A4 sheet for each topic, "what the exam expects" and exam technique sheets, and mini papers); stage 2 is grades 4 and 5 and stage 3 is Higher, grades 6 to 9, built the same way. A pupil working alone (for example homeschooled) uses the "My next step" page, climb.html, which checks where they are and then always says what to do next, from grade 1 up to grade 9, with no login. If working online is trouble, the same pupil can download a whole stage as a printable pack (pack.html: a plan to tick off, every sheet in study order, the answers at the back), made in the browser so it costs nothing to host. Every question shows its marks, and every answer is set out like a mark scheme so pupils learn where marks come from. One question bank makes the online and paper questions. Grades shown are a planning guide, not a predicted grade, and questions are PopSpell's own, not past papers.

**GCSE English exam climb** (Steven's request, November 2026): the maths climb's shape for GCSE English Language in England: three stages (grade 1 to 3, grades 4 and 5, grades 6 to 9) of eight topics, each with online questions that mark themselves, an exam practice sheet with levels and a model answer, and a lesson; each stage has a check, a tracker, what the exam expects, two timed writing tasks with a self-check list and model, and two mini papers. Literature (novel, Shakespeare, poetry, essay) is four extra topics. Extended answers can't be marked automatically, so they are self-assessed against plain-words levels, a checklist and a model. Texts are PopSpell's own or out of copyright; the papers described are AQA's. A self-guided page (`english-climb.html`) does for a pupil at home what `climb.html` does for maths.

**SQA English exam climb** (November 2026): the same English climb for Scotland's senior phase: National 4 skills, National 5 and Higher English, eight topics a stage, with SQA's papers (RUAE, Critical Reading) and marking (2 marks a reference with its comment, the 10-mark Scottish text question, the 20-mark critical essay), two folio tasks a stage marked out of 15, mini papers, packs and `english-climb.html?course=sqa`. Because most Scottish set texts are in copyright, Scottish text practice uses Burns and Stevenson and teaches a method that works for any set text. Advanced Higher English (unseen textual analysis, the Literary Study essay, the dissertation and portfolio) was added as stage 4 at Steven's request, with unseen texts out of copyright or PopSpell's own. Not yet: the spoken language assessment.

**SQA maths exam climb** (Steven's request, November 2026): after the GCSE climb, Steven asked for the Scotland equivalent. Scotland's senior phase (S4 to S6) now has a staged climb built the same way, with SQA's course names and marking: National 4 skills (what National 5 assumes), then National 5, then Higher, then Advanced Higher (added later in November 2026, with four packs in `pack.html`). It is a new "Senior phase" level in Scotland's Library, after Fourth level; a pupil's year group still doesn't set their working level. Exam facts follow SQA's course specifications; questions are PopSpell's own, never past papers, and scores are a planning guide, not an estimate of an SQA grade.

**A level and the sixth form** (Steven's request, November 2026, to fill the home-school packs' gaps): Years 12 and 13 can be chosen as England year groups. With no national curriculum after Year 11, PopSpell follows the Department for Education's A level subject content, which every exam board shares. A level Mathematics is a two-stage climb (Year 12, the AS content; Year 13), built and marked like the SQA climb, with a self-guided page, download packs and a home-school pack for each year. A level English Literature and English Language are one stage each of the English climb, marked in bands with model answers, on public-domain literature and PopSpell's own transcripts and articles, each with its own home-school pack. Questions are PopSpell's own, never board papers.

**Adult English** (Steven's decision, November 2026): Sara also teaches English online to adults from other countries, so PopSpell has a separate Adult English area (`english.html`). It is a teaching tool, not part of the pupil journey: lessons are labelled by CEFR level (A1 to C1) rather than school level, use British English and a grown-up look with no mascot, and keep no records, so they need no login and add no running cost. Each lesson follows the shape of Sara's own adult lesson sheet and the published lessons she uses: numbered steps for a warm-up chat, vocabulary (sorting with "add more", or matching at higher levels), a reading with true/false and open questions, one grammar point with a gap fill, practice (word jumble, choose, correct the mistakes), a role-play with a cool-down game, and homework, with an extra challenge on most steps. It goes one step per screen for screen sharing or as a link for the learner, and prints a student copy and a teacher copy with answers and notes on A4. There are 15 lessons, three at each level from A1 to C1 (Steven asked for the full set in November 2026 rather than waiting for Sara to review a sample). Lessons are PopSpell's own writing; third-party material is a guide to format and level only.

Feature counts and coverage should be checked against the current content and build checks before making claims about them.
