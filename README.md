# PopSpell

A free teaching and practice site for spelling, reading, writing and numeracy, with pupil activities, teacher planning and progress records, and ways to continue work at home. Sounds-first spelling is its foundation: children link each sound (phoneme) to the letters that write it (grapheme), rather than memorising the shape of a whole word.

For the purpose, audience and principles that guide new work, see [PopSpell's product brief](PRODUCT.md). This README describes the features and how the site is built.

## How a word works

1. **Look, listen and segment.** The word is shown on screen, with its sound buttons, while it's read aloud. Below it, a blank box represents each sound. The pupil says the word slowly and taps a box for every sound heard.
2. **Map and spell.** The word hides when it's time to spell. The sound boxes stay visible while the pupil types the graphemes, saying each sound as they write it. New-word challenges are never shown first, because they check whether the pupil can use the rule.
3. **Feedback that teaches.** After a wrong try, the boxes show what they wrote, sound by sound: green for right, orange for the sound to fix — e.g. *rane* for *rain* shows `r ✓ | a…e ✗ | n ✓`. If the mistake is in the sound the list is practising, the list's tip is shown. They get **3 tries**.
4. **If all tries are missed**, the word is shown with the missed sounds in orange and spelled out sound by sound (Simultaneous Oral Spelling): *"rain. R. A I. N. rain."* Sound markers show one-letter sounds, digraphs and split digraphs.
5. **Results** — stars for each word, every wrong try, and a **"Sounds to practise"** panel listing which graphemes went wrong on the first try and in which words (e.g. `a–e: cake, snake`). It can be printed, and there's a button to practise only the tricky words again.

### Sentences, new words and review

- **Every word comes with a sentence**, read aloud as in a spelling test: *"sail… The boat had a big white sail… sail."* On the spelling screen it's shown with a blank, so homophones (sail / sale) are never a guess. Sentences live in `sentences.js` and can be edited.
- **New-word challenges** end each list: 2–3 words with the same pattern that the pupil hasn't practised and never sees first (e.g. *flake, crate, grape* after the a–e list). They show whether the pupil can *use* the rule, not just remember 15 words. They're set per list with `check: [...]` in `words.js`.
- **Spaced review.** Every word a pupil practises is scheduled to come back: missed words the next day, then after 3, 7, 14 and 30 days as they get them right first time. The home page shows "🔁 6 words to practise again today".
- **My sounds** is each pupil's collection of every sound in the lists. A sound earns its ⭐ when it's spelled right first time at least 4 times, on 2 different days, and the last 3 times in a row. New stars are celebrated at the end of a list, and tapping a sound lets the pupil practise just that sound.

- **My bests.** Pupils only ever compete with themselves. Every word and question they try is a "go", right or wrong, so effort counts as well as accuracy. The results screen celebrates a new best score on a list or activity (most right first time), a best week ever (most goes), and weeks in a row of practice. When it isn't a best, it says how close they are ("4 more to beat your best week!"). There are no leaderboards. The **🏅 My bests** page shows each best score with a "Beat it" button.

For class work, pupils tap their name on a prepared shared tablet, or open their personal home link, then enter a four-digit PIN. Review words, stars and bests are kept under that pupil's login on the device and with the class. They follow the pupil to another device, such as a school tablet and a laptop at home. Both copies are merged, keeping the newest of everything. Results also go to their teacher. A device outside a class can still use a local name for standalone practice.

Ready-made phonics lists follow the **EA Literacy stages** Sara's school uses: the pupil journey is **EA Stage 1** (single sounds and digraphs, Blocks 1 to 9, Early level), **EA Stage 2** (vowel teams and split digraphs, Blocks 1 to 8, First level) and **EA Stage 3** (more ways to write sounds, Blocks 1 to 9, First level), each with Sara's phoneme word lists word for word in her block order, red words included. Each list has its `block`; the pupil home page and the Library show a stage a block at a time ("EA Stage 2 · Block 3"). PopSpell's own practice that the EA lists don't have sits at the end of the stage it fits, as **More practice** (`more: true`): blends and nk in Stage 1; ar, or, er/ir/ur, air, ear, -ing, -s/-es and -le in Stage 2. PopSpell's earlier lists that teach the same sounds with other words (short vowels, vowel teams, soft c and g, its tricky words) are kept out of the journey in `window.MORE_LISTS`: like England's statutory words, teachers still find them in the Library, Assign and the Programme, old links and results keep working, and a pupil moves on within them. bow and row (ow as in owl) and wind (i as in kind) are left out of Sara's lists online, as the voice can only say one of their sounds; they're in the ow (as in snow) and w lists. Every EA block has a ready lesson ("EA Stage 1 Block 1" to "EA Stage 3 Block 9"). New devices practise words in the listed order by default. The two **y** lists always move from simpler words to harder ones, even if a device has the optional word-mixing setting on.

After EA Stage 3 the journey goes on into **EA Stages 4 to 7** (*phonics into spelling*, P4 to P7): the same 36 sound blocks at every stage, one sound and all its spellings per block, in three stops of rising difficulty: **EA Stages 4-5** (the simpler code, plus Sara's Set 1 common words as More practice), **EA Stages 5-6** (more spellings, longer words) and **EA Stages 6-7** (the complex code), all Second level. Lists are `ea45-…`, `ea56-…` and `ea67-…` (one per block; Blocks 1 to 6 are Sara's EA word lists, the rest PopSpell's own words for the graphemes in her proposed coverage). Block 35 (the ch in *loch*, Scots words) has no online list, as the voice can't say it: its lesson has the "Scots words with ch" sheet. Each block has a ready lesson ("EA Stages 4 to 7 Block 1"), a phoneme story of PopSpell's own in `stories.js` (`block`), and its lists' practice sheets open with the EA spelling log (Elkonin boxes) and a phoneme chart. Help has the EA two-week plan.

Lists and activities carry a **Curriculum for Excellence level** and **organiser** where applicable (e.g. *Literacy · Writing · Tools for writing*, *Numeracy · Money*). Teachers' reports are grouped by these. They're a starting guide; teachers should check them against their own planning.

**Game plan:** Sound Hopper is the first game mode. It uses a character, a musical river, beat tapping, a tile bridge, and a crossing payoff. The six rounds have large touch targets and no timer or public leaderboard. The score rewards accurate mapping; speed does not affect progress. A teacher can assign a level like any other activity. Try it with pupils before expanding to modes such as an endless music run or a word-building sandbox, and check whether they can explain the sounds they hear.

**Sound Switch:** Pip explores a workshop with six doors per level. The first level changes one CVC sound at a time (*cat → cot → cut → cup*). The second swaps digraph sound blocks (*shop → chop*, *cash → catch*). The third adds and removes sounds (*stop → top*, *star → start*). The target word is heard but its spelling is hidden until a pupil solves the door or requests the written hint. Tap a sound block or addition gap, then tap or drag a grapheme tile. After two mistakes the right place glows. A correct change opens the door, displays the new sound map and reads the whole word aloud. All source and target words have recorded audio. The Scottish or English setting changes any relevant sound boxes, including *star*, before play begins. Results record each wrong place or tile, hint use and time spent per door; time is shown to teachers for context but never affects stars. Teacher previews save no pupil results. Teachers can assign any level normally or as a one-day quick game. Sound Hopper and Sound Switch now use the same Pip images as the rest of the site.

**Counting Canopy:** The eight-clearing forest quest alternates four challenge types, raises the numbers in its second half and ends with a Forest Explorer celebration. Five six-clearing levels give focused practice with one skill. Firefly Flash briefly shows a structured group of 1–6 dots; children can replay it indefinitely or keep it visible as a clue. At the river, children pick two visual firefly groups that make a target number; one correct split builds the whole bridge and Pip crosses it. Hidden Fireflies asks for the missing part using a ten-frame, with a separate First-level version to 20. The forest fork compares two groups, including equal groups. Touch controls and spoken instructions support young pupils; there is no answer timer. Clues and wrong choices trigger targeted feedback, while play continues without a failure screen. The teacher preview saves nothing; real pupil sessions save strategies, difficulties and elapsed thinking time for teacher review. Each level can be assigned, including as a one-day quick game.

**Number work builds up:** every number set runs from easier to harder (bigger numbers and crossing a ten count as harder; clock times go o'clock, half past, quarter past, quarter to). There are no throwaway questions such as 7 − 7 or 0 + ? = 10, and the within-20 sets always cross ten or stay in the teens, so they really are harder than within 10. Starting checks take questions from the middle of each set. **Counting Canopy: forest run to 20** is the First-level forest, with numbers up to 20 and teen numbers compared on a twenty-frame.

**Pip's Forest rewards:** Every completed Counting Canopy clearing earns one glow seed, including a clearing completed with clues. Children can spend seeds on a lantern, flower garden, owl treehouse or rainbow bridge, then see those additions in their own forest from the home page. Rewards are saved locally and travel with the pupil's existing class progress sync. Teacher preview never earns or spends seeds. No paid service or new Firestore rule is needed.

The sound games follow the class's accent: **Scottish** (the default: *car* is `c · a · r`, *her* is `h · e · r`) or **English** (*car* is `c · ar`). It changes the sound count and tiles. Pupils can't change it. On a device that isn't in a class, a signed-in teacher can switch it on the sound game's start page to compare.

The stories and their questions are in [`passages.js`](passages.js) and can be edited there. New or changed stories are recorded in Eponine's voice automatically.

### ⚡ Quick drills

Twenty-eight one-page paper drills, Early to Fourth level, for a tutoring session, titled **Quick drill: …** so a Library search for *drill* finds them all. They follow the shape of the tutoring sheets Sara uses: a dark strip saying it is a quick drill, its level and about how long it takes, a rule or word box where one helps, then two or three short numbered tasks on one skill, with an answers page. Early: missing letters, circle the different one, sh/ch/th, read, draw and find. First: words from the word box, ai/ee/oa/igh, add an e, and/but/because/so, sentence endings, yesterday I… (past tense), read and answer. Second: plurals and -ing, un/dis/re/mis/pre, syllables, apostrophes, build a better sentence. Third: number prefixes, adding suffixes, homophones, although/unless/while, words in a text, cause and effect. Fourth: Greek and Latin roots, tricky plurals, colons and semicolons, fact/opinion/bias, word choice, formal or informal. Each print picks a fresh mix from the drill's pool, so a pupil can do the same drill again next week; the answers page always matches. Every word is PopSpell's own. They live in [`sheets/10-quick-drills.js`](sheets/10-quick-drills.js) and [`sheets/11-quick-drills-older.js`](sheets/11-quick-drills-older.js), each attached to the ready lesson it practises; `tests/quick-drills.test.mjs` builds each one many times.

There are 19 more for secondary maths ([`sheets/11-maths-drills.js`](sheets/11-maths-drills.js)), with the strip saying *Years 7 to 9* or *Years 10 and 11 (GCSE)*: a box of what to remember, then lettered questions in two columns, with every answer on the answers page by task and letter (1a, 1b …). Years 7 to 9: negative numbers, order of operations, fractions (the four operations), percentages without a calculator, ratio, simplify/expand/factorise, solving equations, angle facts, area and volume, sequences and the nth term, averages and range. Years 10 and 11: indices and standard form, factorising and solving quadratics, simultaneous equations and inequalities, straight-line graphs, Pythagoras and trigonometry, multipliers and compound interest, combined events, bounds and error intervals. Every print is new numbers; `tests/secondary-maths.test.mjs` checks each has an answer for every question.

### 🎓 GCSE English exam climb (England)

The same climb for GCSE English Language, grade 1 to 9, with Literature where it's practical. Three stages of eight topics: **stage 1, grade 1 to 3** (`e1-…`: finding information, inference, words and effects, summary, sentences, spelling, paragraphs, letters/articles/speeches), **stage 2, grades 4 and 5** (`e2-…`: language, structure, evaluating, comparing, punctuation, sentence variety, stories, arguments) and **stage 3, grades 6 to 9** (`e3-…`: perceptive analysis, whole-text structure, critical evaluation, 19th-century context, vocabulary, crafted sentences, imagery, rhetoric), plus four **Literature** topics (`lit-…`).

### 📚 My stories: decodable stories matched to each pupil

[`stories.js`](stories.js) holds 14 short decodable stories for the EA stages (4 for Stage 1, 6 for Stage 2, 4 for Stage 3), each with three questions.

- **Matched automatically.** The site sounds out every word in a story to find the sounds it needs. **My stories** (on the pupil's home page) then sorts them:
  - **Ready to read:** every sound has been spelled right at least once. ⭐ means every sound is one of the pupil's star sounds.
  - **Nearly ready:** one or two sounds still to learn.
  - **Coming later:** everything else.
- **Tricky words count too.** Early ones (the, to, said, was, they…) are allowed from the start. A story can list others in `tricky`, and the pupil needs to have practised those first.
- **The reader.** Pupils read the story themselves before the questions, with sound buttons under every word (dots, lines and arcs, which can be switched off) and a ♥ on tricky words. Tapping a word reads it aloud for when they're stuck. During the questions the story can be opened again with its sound buttons, but not read aloud, so the child has to read it.
- **Adding a story.** A new story in `stories.js` is matched to pupils with no extra work. Keep it to the sounds of its stage.

Stories can also be set as work from the teacher area.

## Teachers

Teachers sign in **on the site** with their Google account (👩‍🏫 **Teacher**, top right). The owner invites each teacher by email. Once signed in, a teacher has:

- **Work library:** everything in one place, found by CfE level (Early, First, Second, with P stages), subject (Literacy or Maths), strand (Spelling, Reading, Writing, Number, Time, Fractions and so on) and type, or by search. Starting checks sit at the top under "Find their level". Every card has **Test** (as a pupil, nothing saved), **Assign** (tick pupils in the chosen class, or Everyone) and **Print**: spelling lists use the five spelling sheets; activities, stories and dictation print fresh questions with an answers page, and listening work and dictation get an adult's page with what to read aloud. Games are online only. The filters are remembered on each device.
- **Classes:** each class has a short code (e.g. `K7P2QX`), but that code does not open pupil records. Teachers issue a personal login for each roster pupil, agree a PIN with them and prepare shared tablets with a setup link.
- **Live class:** every pupil working right now: their list, how far through, who's finding it hard, and 🙋 **Help** requests. It updates instantly.
- **Class page:** the first teacher page. One card per pupil shows the work set (add it with a search, remove it with ×), **▶ Start lesson** for the pupil (**▶ Open pupil screen** when nothing is set yet: a new tab on the teacher's computer, whose results save under the teacher's sign-in), and their recent results, each opening to show every word. Pupils on other devices type the class code and their first name. The other planning and record pages sit under **More tools**, folded away until the teacher opens it (remembered per device). Until the teacher hides it, the Class page starts with **A lesson in four steps** (add pupils, Plan next lesson, Start lesson, Lesson summary), ticking off the first three as they happen. **🧭 Plan next lesson** shows last time's score, the words to review and a suggested next step, then sets the online work and prints the practice sheet for the same list.
- **Pupil tabs:** the Class page's **▶ Start lesson** button (**▶ Open pupil screen** when no work is set) opens a pupil-only tab, straight on the first work set (once per tab, with `start=1` in the link) (no teacher menu, and only the assigned work when the class is set that way). Each tab remembers its own pupil (sessionStorage), so two tabs on one computer never swap names; the device still remembers the last pupil for a new tab.
- **Lesson summary:** a **📝 Lesson summary** button on each pupil card drafts a note from the child's latest online results; the teacher edits the lesson notes and the practice at home, then **📨 Send to family** saves it to the Diary, puts the dated notes and practice at the top of the child's parent page (making the link if needed) and offers **✉️ Email the family** (a `mailto:` to the saved parent email in the teacher's own email app, so it costs nothing), **📱 Share** on phones and **📋 Copy**. The teacher's plan for next time stays private in the Diary; **Save to diary only** shares nothing. The parent page never shows plans or the next lesson date, since those can change. A Parents' night report has an option (off by default) to include diary notes from the period, so end-of-term reports can draw on them.
- **My folder:** each Library card has a **☆ Save** button, and **⭐ My folder** shows only the saved resources at any level. The list is kept in the browser (localStorage) for that teacher on that device.
- **Lesson timer:** a **⏱ Lesson timer** button on the Class page opens a teacher-only countdown for a paired lesson (20 to 60 minutes). It chimes and shows who swaps halfway, keeps the screen awake where the browser allows, and shows nothing on pupils' screens.
- **Practice sheets:** one A4 page for every spelling list (the default sheet in the Library and Plans & sheets), in the phoneme colours used with magnetic letters. Each has the read, build and write table, one or two activities chosen for that list from thirteen kinds (missing sound, circle the right spelling, finish the sentence, word hunt, mixed-up sounds, code breaker, count the sounds, word boxes, sorting two spellings of one sound, spot the mistake, speed read), and the child's own writing. Three age looks: younger (P1–P4) with playful themes, middle (P5–P7) with puzzle-book themes, older (S1 up) plain. The name line is always blank, so teachers can keep copies.
- **Quick trial:** a child tries one piece of work on the teacher's own device with no class, login or PIN. The teacher chooses the work and types the child's name; PopSpell signs the teacher out, keeps the child on that work, and saves the dated result. After signing in again, the teacher sees it under **Trial results** (kept under their own account, apart from class records) and deletes it by hand.
- **Assignments and plans:** search for the work, tick the pupils or *Everyone*, and assign it. **Try it first** plays through the pupil view; teacher previews do not save pupil results or change progress.
- **🌟 New pupil: profile and assessment** (Pupils tab). A guided flow for a new child, in three steps:
  1. **About the child:** first name, year group, parent email, things to bear in mind (quick choices such as dyslexia, EAL or anxious about learning) and what they enjoy. Only teachers can see these.
  2. **Pip's Sound Quest,** a full starting assessment the child plays as a game. The teacher issues their login, then an assessment link opens the PIN screen and Pip's map. There are five islands:
     - 👂 **Listening Lagoon:** rhyme, first sounds and counting sounds, all spoken.
     - 📖 **Reading Reef:** Pip says a word and the child taps it among near-misses that change one sound (*ship / shop / chip*). It's adaptive through the phonics stages.
     - ✏️ **Spelling Sands:** the adaptive spelling check.
     - 📚 **Story Cove:** a story at the child's reading level from Reading Reef, read alone, with questions.
     - 🔢 **Number Peak:** the adaptive number check.

     No marks are shown: every answer gets the same friendly sparkle. Each island stops by itself when it gets too hard and ends with a sticker. Islands can be spread across lessons. About 15–25 minutes in all.
  3. **Report and first lessons:** a professional **starting assessment report** to print or save as a PDF. It gives the CfE level the child is working within for literacy and numeracy; for each area a status (Secure, Developing or Needs support), what was found and a next step; recommended first lists; the child's profile; space for the teacher's comments; and a note that levels are indicative and need teacher judgement. Spelling and Number Peak feed **Next steps** directly. The flow also offers to email the home login.
  Every pupil card also has an **Assessment and report** button. The quest can be set as work too ("Pip's Sound Quest"), and assessment work is kept out of the CfE overview and progress figures.
- **Starting checks** (🧭 on the home page, or set one as work): a placement check for a new pupil's first lesson, about 10 minutes, with one try per question and no marks shown.
  - **Spelling** uses new words the pupil hasn't practised. It tries a few lists spread through each stage, and goes through a stage list by list only once a word is missed.
  - **Numbers** asks two questions from each of the core number, money, time and fraction activities, from Early level up.
  - **Both stop after 3 gaps.** Next steps then suggests starting at the first gap and names the others. Checks are left out of the Curriculum for Excellence overview, because they deliberately go past what the pupil can do yet.
- **Lesson programme (Programme tab):** a lesson-by-lesson programme for each pupil, started from their Sound Quest, starting checks, last results or year group, and kept on the pupil record (`programme`: current spelling list and maths activity, focus, lesson number, last 30 lessons). Each lesson rotates **with you / on the device / on paper**:
  - **Warm-up:** a sound game for their stage, or a Counting Canopy game for maths only.
  - **Teach the sounds** with the sound map sheet, then **spell the list**.
  - **A matched story:** a decodable story for their stage, or comprehension later on.
  - **Number facts** they've already secured, then **teach and practise** the maths, with a "talk about it" prompt.
  - **Dictation**, then **story time, praise and an idea for home**.
  - The minutes fit the lesson length (30, 45, 60 or 90). Each lesson has a learning intention, success criteria and its CfE Experiences and Outcomes codes.
  - **Put on the device** schedules the online parts for the lesson date, sets homework and updates the parent page and calendar. **Print lesson pack** prints the plan and every sheet. **Lesson done** shows results since the last lesson, suggests moving on at 80% right first time, and the teacher decides.
- **Ready lessons (`lessons.js`):** lessons across CfE literacy and numeracy at Early, First, Second, Third and Fourth level, with England curriculum lessons and links where appropriate. Each has a learning intention, success criteria, vocabulary, four timed parts (start 5, teach 10, practise 10, check 5), linked PopSpell work, a home idea, and an understanding check (`l.understand`, `l.lookFor`): a question to ask and what a pupil who understands will say. Scotland's view sets this out as Know, do, understand; England's shows it as Check understanding, under the national curriculum statements. Pupil sheets printed from a lesson start with its goal ("I am learning to…") and first "I can…". In the Library (type **Ready lesson**) they open, print with all their sheets, and assign their online work. The Programme suggests them for each pupil's check gaps and current work.
- **Expected vs working level:** with a year group set, the Programme shows the CfE level expected for it (Early by the end of P1, First by P4, Second by P7, Third by S3) against the level of the pupil's current literacy and maths work: below, as or beyond expected.
- **CfE outcome codes:** every activity, spelling list and dictation carries its Experiences and Outcomes codes (`PopActivities.eo` / `tag(id).eo` in `activities.js`), shown on library cards, in lesson plans and searchable. These are the current codes: the Curriculum Improvement Cycle replaces Es and Os and benchmarks with a Know-Do-Understand model (Early to Third Level from August 2028, Fourth Level and the senior phase from August 2029). First full draft frameworks were shared in November 2026 and the final version is due in May 2027, so these codes are to be mapped to Know, Do, Understand statements then.
- **Paper worksheets (`paper-sheets.js`, `window.PopPaper`):** 192 worksheets for the ready lessons taught with objects, talk or writing (choosing and reviewing texts, sharing news, finding information, group talk and discussion, notes from listening, spoken and written fact and opinion, sharing and halves, comparing measures, position, area, grid references and symmetry, order of operations, Roman numerals, story and recount planners, fact or opinion, punctuation, persuasive writing, story mountain, talk planner, handwriting speed, and a phonics reading sheet).   - **England-only practice**, shown to pupils only when assigned (`assignedOnly` areas in `activities.js`): England's statutory spelling words in `words.js` (`window.ENGLAND_LISTS`: Year 1 and 2 common exception words, whose `tricky` words show whole with a ♥, plus `trickyEnglish` ones with the English accent; and the Years 3–4 and 5–6 word lists, kept whole), **grammar and punctuation by year** (25 sets from English Appendix 2, ids `eg-…`), and **times tables check practice** (`n-mtc`: 25 questions built to the Standards and Testing Agency's form rules, 6 seconds each, one go, 3-second pause, not read aloud). The statutory lists stay out of the phonics stages, and a programme moves on within whichever sequence its list is in.
  - **Phonics check practice, marked live** (Pupils → a pupil's card → 🔤): 40 words in two sections (real words from the phonics stages, curated pseudo-words with an alien), marked ✓/✗ by the teacher with keys 1 and 2, optionally tapping the missed sound. The teacher's screen writes the current word to `classes/{c}/drive/{fingerprint}`, which only the class teacher can write and the signed-in pupil's device can only `get` (the personal link with `?then=phonics` opens the PIN screen, then `#/phonics` watches that document). Results save as a teacher-marked session (`source: "phonics-check"`). There's also **Show on my screen** for screen sharing.
  - **Where the data comes from:** `england.js` holds 748 statements taken word for word from the Department for Education's documents: the primary national curriculum for English and maths, the EYFS framework's Early Learning Goals, the key stage 3 programmes of study (so probability, which England starts in key stage 3, is labelled as such), and the key stage 4 programmes of study (year `"10-11"`, `tools/england_ks4.py`) with the GCSE subject content's own detail where it spells a statement out (the GCSE English language scope of study and the GCSE maths formulae; the script's header lists exactly what's included), plus which statements each activity, list and lesson practises. To rebuild it (for example for the revised curriculum due in 2027, first taught from September 2028), follow the steps at the top of `tools/build_england.py`; the extracted statements are kept in `tools/england-statements.json`. The mapping lives in that script, and the build stops if a statement doesn't exist or a piece of work is mapped twice. In October 2026 every Reception to Year 6 statement was reviewed and linked to work that genuinely teaches it, adding 30 England lessons, 13 sheets and the `england-maths` and `england-english` activity areas (set by the teacher, like the grammar) for what was missing; the 36 headings are covered by their bullets. Every key stage 4 statement is linked to a key stage 4 lesson (its eight headings through their bullets), and the build stops if one isn't. The same goes for every key stage 3 maths statement: four England lessons for Years 7 to 9 (`content/lessons/14-england-ks3.json`, sheets in `sheets/05b-england-ks3.js`) cover the few the Third and Fourth level lessons don't (algebraic notation and double brackets, estimating from graphs and geometric sequences, one quantity as a fraction of another, and Venn diagrams). A lesson's first statement sets its year, so new links widen its year range but never move it. England lessons with no CfE match at Early to Second level (ratio) have no codes and say it's Third level in CfE.
- **Next steps:** one card per pupil showing their next lesson, what's planned, how they did last time, what to work on, their starting check, and a suggestion that follows the teaching order. **Plan it** schedules it in one click. Pupils with nothing planned stand out.
- **Plans & worksheets** has three paths. **Online work** can be tested and scheduled by date. **Paper worksheets** can be previewed, printed or saved as a PDF, then marked on the site after the child completes them; marks and missed graphemes appear alongside online results. **Quick game** lets a teacher test a game and assign it to a pupil for today only, useful when they finish early.
- Paper options include a sound-mapping worksheet with one box per phoneme, dictation, a teacher key, a **handwriting** sheet (model, grey trace, dotted trace, own go on four-line ruling), a **sentence** sheet (fill the blank, copy the sentence, then write one of their own), and **letter formation** (all letters, or one family at a time: curly, long ladder, down and bounce, zigzag). Handwriting sheets use the reading font (Sassoon or Andika).
- **More than the right answer on paper:** activity sheets add a part after the questions (`sheetExtras` in `teacher.js`): a number line or ten frame and two story problems for adding, taking away and bonds; a show-it-another-way box for other number work; a sentence of the pupil's own for writing and grammar; a retelling, evidence and a prediction for stories. Sound-mapping and dictation sheets add the list's `check` words as **new words** (England's statutory lists have none, so they stay exact). Answer pages end with the likely mistake, a question to ask, a talk prompt and the ready lesson to teach next (`diagnosisHTML`); the Words to say page has an error-type column (S G W P H). Each pupil sheet and its answers page share a sheet code. Work from England's curriculum prints with an "England's national curriculum" label on every page type, pupil and adult. Paper sheets made for a lesson end their adult page with the same "After marking" section, built from the lesson's understanding check (`afterMarkingHTML`). The print previews have **Leave the level off the pupil's sheet** for older pupils working at an earlier level (`ws-hide-level`; the adult's pages keep it). Practice is matched to each lesson's level: `n-money-10`, `n-time-oclock` and `m-patterns-early` for Early, `n-round-10-100` for First, thousandths in `n-decimals`, and `n-best-value` for budgets, best value and profit and loss.
- **"Pupils only see the work I set":** a per-class setting (in Classes) that hides the rest of the library from that class's devices.
- **Accent for sounds:** a per-class setting (in Classes). Choose Scottish (the r is sounded: c·a·r) for pupils in Scotland, or English (c·ar) for pupils in England.
- **Lesson diary:** record what a pupil did, what they need next, and optionally schedule their next list and lesson date. Diary notes stay in the teacher area.
- **Pupil roster:** add pupils, lesson dates, optional parent email and reminder preference. The day before a lesson, prepare a reminder email and save the homework worksheet as a PDF to attach.
- **🏠 Home login:** after the teacher issues a login, the pupil has a short random link (`#/p/K7Q4M9T2BX`). At home a parent opens it and the child enters their PIN. On a prepared shared tablet, the child taps their name tile and enters the same PIN. **Switch pupil** signs them out, and an idle pupil screen locks after 15 minutes. A teacher can replace a forgotten PIN; that issues a new link and revokes the old login's access.
- **✉️ Branded emails:** **Email home login** and the lesson reminder open a preview of a PopSpell-branded email, with logo, colours, a big button, the homework and the parent page link. **Copy email** copies it with its pictures and formatting. **Open email app** starts an email with the parent's address and subject filled in; paste and send. No email service is involved, so it stays free.
- **👪 Parent pages:** for any pupil in the roster, **Make a parent link** creates a private page (`parent.html#…`) for the family.
  - **Sharing:** copy the link and send it by WhatsApp, text or email. Parents need no account or password.
  - **What's on it:** the pupil's first name, a note for home, what's coming up, sounds to practise, sounds getting there and sounds known well, recent work, Curriculum for Excellence progress, and tips for helping at home. The private lesson diary, parent email and other pupils' results are never on it.
  - **Sounds:** these are worked out from the results that reach the teacher, using the same rule as My sounds, and cover about the last four months. Starting checks are left out.
  - **Staying up to date:** the page updates itself whenever the teacher area loads newer work (Next steps), when **Plan it** changes what's coming up, or with **Update now**.
  - **Turning it off:** **Make a new link** replaces it and **Turn off** stops it. Either way, the old link stops working at once.
  - **Cost:** each parent visit is one read on the free Firebase plan.
- **Pupils:** each pupil's results over the last week, month, term or year, the sounds they miss most, a **spreadsheet download** of every word (opens in Google Sheets or Excel), a **Curriculum for Excellence overview** (each pupil's status per organiser: *Secure in practice* = 85%+ right first time on 10+ questions over 2+ days, *Developing* = 60%+, otherwise *Needs support*, each with its question and day counts), and a printable **📄 Progress report** per pupil showing the evidence behind each status, with boxes for the teacher's own evidence and comment. *Secure in practice* is evidence for the teacher's judgement, never a CfE level or national curriculum judgement; parent pages and parents' night reports say so too. A Scottish pupil's work on the few England-only items has its own row, "England's curriculum (practice only)", outside the CfE organisers.
- **🗓️ Lessons to a calendar:** **Sync to my Google Calendar** puts lessons straight into the Google Calendar of the account the teacher signed in with (Google asks permission the first time), and from then on saving a lesson date on a pupil card, or planning one in Next steps, moves the lesson there too. One-off Google Cloud setup and verification are in [firebase/SETUP.md](firebase/SETUP.md#google-calendar-sync-free). For Outlook or Apple Calendar, each pupil card's **Add to calendar** and the class's **Lessons to my calendar** download an **.ics file**. Choose the lesson length and whether it repeats every week until the summer holidays; lessons are in UK time, so they stay right through the clock changes, and adding a lesson again after its date changes moves it rather than doubling it. Tomorrow's reminder email also has an **Add this lesson to Google Calendar** link for parents.
- **Updates itself:** every 5 minutes (and when a tablet wakes up) PopSpell compares the build number it loaded (`<meta name="popspell-build">`, written by `npm run build` from every file's fingerprint) with the one popspell.com serves. Pupils' pages load the new version at their next move between pages, never mid-list, mid-game or mid-story; the teacher area shows a **Refresh** bar instead. **Always run `npm run build` before publishing**: it stamps every file with a fingerprint of its contents and updates the build number, so browsers fetch exactly what changed and devices notice the update. Each report has headline figures (right first time, sessions and weeks practised, sounds now secure, best run of weeks), **month-by-month charts** and a table, **what's changed since the last report**, the **starting assessment against now**, each CfE area with a **trend** (▲ improving, ▶ steady, ▼ slipping) and its status over the last 8 weeks, highlighted **strengths** and **areas needing further support** (with the sounds still being missed), suggested **next steps**, and a typed or handwritten **teacher's comment**. The report is worked out fresh from the full history each time, so nothing has to be kept up to date.

## Changing the word lists

Open `words.js` on GitHub, click the ✏️ pencil icon, make your changes and press **Commit changes**. The site updates in a minute or two.

```js
{ id: "a-e", title: "a–e (silent e and long a)", tip: "The silent e makes the a say its name: cap → cape.",
  words: ["cake", "make", "wake", "gave", "brave"] },
```

- `id` — short, lower case, no spaces, unique (used in the web address, e.g. `.../#/list/a-e`).
- `title` — what pupils see. `tip` — optional hint.
- `words` — each word in "quotes", separated by commas.
- `focus` — optional: the graphemes the list is practising, e.g. `focus: ["oi", "oy"]`. Without it, the site uses the graphemes found in most of the list's words.

### Sounds (graphemes)

**Scottish or English accent** (⚙️ Settings, Scottish by default). In a Scottish accent the r is sounded, so the site splits *car* as `c · a · r`, *her* as `h · e · r`, *hair* as `h · ai · r` and *care* as `c · a–e · r`. The English setting treats ar, er, ir, or, ur, air, are, ear and eer as one sound each (`c · ar`). This setting changes the sound buttons, sound boxes, worksheets, My sounds and which stories are ready. The r-vowel lists get their own tips for each accent (`tipScottish` in `words.js`). **The teacher sets it for each class** in Teacher → Classes, and every pupil device in the class follows it from the next time PopSpell opens. Pupils can't change it. A device that isn't in a class uses ⚙️ Settings.

The site splits words into sounds by itself, and gets the common patterns right. Open a list and click **Words in this list** to check the sound buttons. If a word is split wrongly (usually tricky words), write its sounds with dashes, and a split digraph as `a_e`, `i_e`, `o_e`, `u_e` or `e_e`:

```
"sc-e_e-n"   "one-"
```

A dash at the end (`"one-"`) keeps the whole word as one chunk with no sound buttons (and a ♥ if it's a tricky word).

**Tricky words never get sound buttons.** The words on the Tricky words list (the, said, was, they, have, come, some, one, do, you, were, there, could, would, people) are shown whole with a ♥ wherever they appear: in lists, the spelling screen, made lists and stories. Their tricky part doesn't make its usual sound, so a dot or line under it would teach the wrong sound (a line under the *ai* in *said*, say). Every other word keeps its dots, lines and arcs. To add a tricky word, add it to that list in `words.js`, or type it with a dash at the end in your own list.

## Lists for a single lesson

Everything else on the site (lessons, sheets, word lists, stories, questions) is PopSpell's own.

The site uses **Sassoon Primary / Sassoon Infant** wherever it's installed — many school computers already have it. Sassoon is a paid font, so it can't be included in a public website without a web-font licence. Without it, the site uses **Andika**, a free font also designed for early readers (single-storey *a* and *g*).

```js
fontFile: "fonts/SassoonPrimary.woff2"
```

## Voices

### Eponine — the recorded voice (default)

Every word in the lists, every sentence, the letter names and the praise phrases are pre-recorded with **Eponine**, a voice from [Pocket TTS](https://github.com/kyutai-labs/pocket-tts) by Kyutai, based on the VCTK corpus from the University of Edinburgh (both CC BY 4.0, which the credit line at the bottom of the site covers). The recordings are small MP3s in the `audio` folder, so they play instantly on any device.

**New words get recorded automatically.** When `words.js` or `sentences.js` is changed on GitHub, the *Make recordings* action (in the repository's **Actions** tab) records any new or changed words and sentences, and adds them to the site within a few minutes. To record on your own computer instead:

```
pip install pocket-tts lameenc
python tools/make_audio.py
```

**Activities are read out by Eponine too.** Every question, answer choice and explanation is recorded: lines without numbers whole, and lines with numbers joined from recorded pieces and number words (583 is *five · hundred · and · eighty · three*), following the rules in [`speech-text.js`](speech-text.js). Big numbers written with spaces (*4 764 092*) are read as one number, and Roman numerals letter by letter from the letter recordings. `tools/activity_lines.mjs` lists every piece the site can say into `tools/lines.json` (it makes each activity until no new lines turn up, and also covers the Sound Quest story questions, words tapped in a story and the word games' words; [`tools/spoken.mjs`](tools/spoken.mjs) works out what's said), and `make_audio.py` records them; the action does both whenever the content changes. To do it by hand:

```
node tools/activity_lines.mjs
python tools/make_audio.py
```

The device's own voice is only used for what can't be recorded in advance: a child's name, and words in **Make a list** links that aren't in any list.

**Nothing missing:** `npm run audio-check` lists anything the site would have to say in the device voice (a line with an unrecorded piece, a word or sentence without its recording, a missing or empty file, or a recording made from older wording). The action runs it after recording and shows a red cross if anything is still missing.

**Checking the recordings:** open **popspell.com/listen.html** (also linked from Settings). Play each word, sentence and letter, mark any that sound wrong with a note, then copy the list. Those words get fixed in `tools/make_audio.py`: either a new take (`--only crash`), or a different way of writing it for the voice in `WORD_FIXES`. Takes approved by ear go in `APPROVED`, so they're never replaced.

### Device voices (fallback)

When a recording isn't available, or if you pick a different voice in Settings, the site uses the voices built into the device:

- **Microsoft Edge** (Windows) has the most natural British voices (e.g. *Sonia*, *Libby*, *Ryan*).
- **Chrome** uses *Google UK English*.
- **iPad / iPhone**: go to *Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Voices → English → UK* and download an "Enhanced" voice (e.g. *Serena*, *Daniel*, or the Scottish voice *Fiona*), then pick it in the site's Settings.

Speech voices can't reliably say isolated phonemes, so the site reads whole words and letter names — the pupil says the sounds themselves.

## Fast and offline

- **Pupils download only the pupil app.** The teacher area's scripts (`lessons.js`, `england.js`, `paper-sheets.js`, `teacher.js`, together about two-thirds of the site's code) sit in a `<template id="teacherScripts">` in `index.html`, and `app.js` (`loadTeacher`) loads them the first time the teacher area opens.
- **Works offline after sign-in while the page is open.** `sw.js`, the service worker, keeps the pupil app's page and files on the device, so loaded practice can carry on through a connection interruption. A new pupil sign-in or restoring a closed page needs the internet. Files stamped with `?v=` come from the device first (a new stamp replaces the old copy); everything else of PopSpell's comes from the network first, with the kept copy as a fallback. Firebase, Google sign-in and fonts are never stored. `manifest.json` lets the site be added to a tablet's home screen like an app.

## Editing lessons and sheets

- **Ready lessons** live in `content/lessons/`, one JSON file per section (`01-maths-early.json` to `13-england-ks4.json`). Each lesson has its id, level, subject, strand, CfE codes, title, learning intention, success criteria, the four timed steps, the PopSpell work it uses, a home idea, words to use, its understanding check (`understand`, `lookFor`) and, for England's lessons, `england: true` and any Scottish version (`cfe`).
- **Paper sheets** live in `sheets/`, one file per section, each part of one script (`00-start.js` holds the shared helpers, `99-end.js` closes it).
- **CfE outcomes** are listed in `content/curriculum/cfe.json`; England's statements are built into `england.js` by `tools/build_england.py`.
- Then run `npm run build`: it writes `lessons.js` and `paper-sheets.js` from those files and stamps every page. Don't edit `lessons.js` or `paper-sheets.js` directly: the build replaces them, and the checks fail if they don't match.

## Checks

GitHub runs these on every push (`.github/workflows/checks.yml`); a red cross on the repository means something needs fixing. To run them yourself you need [Node.js](https://nodejs.org) (and Python for the England check):

- `npm test`: the unit tests in `tests/`.
- `npm run check`: the content checks (`tools/check.mjs`). Every CfE experience and outcome from Early to Fourth level has a ready lesson (`content/curriculum/cfe.json`, from Education Scotland's documents); every lesson has an understanding check and printable work, and everything it uses exists; every paper sheet belongs to a lesson, has a unique id and title, and builds; and `england.js` matches `tools/build_england.py`, whose own checks make sure every statement exists and every key stage 4 statement is taught.
- `npm run rules`: the Firestore security rules tests, on Firebase's local emulator (needs Java).
- `npm run sheet-check`: prints every worksheet to PDF in a real browser (every spelling sheet type and age, paper sheet, activity, dictation, story and ready lesson pack, with answer pages) and lists any that runs onto an extra A4 page. It needs Playwright (`npm install --no-save playwright && npx playwright install chromium`); `--type paper` checks one kind, `--pdf folder` keeps a PDF of anything that doesn't fit. GitHub runs it too. For a quick look in the preview, `tools/fit-sweep.js` measures pages in the browser console.
